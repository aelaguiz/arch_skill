This week's learnings

The restored screens did recover checkout conversion, where the traffic is right.
Last Monday's promised verdict, on a full mature week: iOS converts 56.4% of started checkouts (79/140), just under the May/June 58 to 66% era, and the newest build reads inside it (iOS 2.1.40: 68.0%, 34/50 this week; 66.1%, 39/59 lifetime). Android on the very same screens converts 20.8% (40/192). So the halving story ends where the mix story begins: today's checkout traffic is paid-heavy Android where spring's was organic-heavy iOS. The lever that is left is Android's funnel and the mix itself, not the screens.

The funnel records kept coming, and the money followed.
332 checkout starts, a new record (prior 301); 119 completed purchases, the second-best week this funnel has recorded (135, week of May 18); gross $1,242.18 on 37 paid transactions, the best of the six weeks we track, with Sunday still loading. 23 first-time payers, 15 of them through trials. And the conveyor filled: 103 trial starts (the most in the series we can pull; spring peaked about 293), while last week's 82 trials are converting at 19.5% so far (16/82).

The paid tier's first cells are honest and thin.
Play vs AI went to 100% with the daily cap live: 213 free players hit the cap wall this week, 6 opened checkout from it, 0 have purchased yet. Three days of data on a brand-new surface is a settling read, not a verdict; but the upgrade moment, not the cap, is clearly where the work is. Repeat play also fell (50.2% of finishers played 2+ games, from 65.6%), and the cap is the obvious candidate mechanism, unproven.

A production flag is code, and deleting one needs the same discipline as deleting code.
Friday's flag cleanup deleted the missions flag; every 2.1.40 user silently stopped earning mission credit for about two days (the day-completer count fell 246 to 182 before the version mix was understood). Restored Saturday, and the deeper fix shipped same week: mission credit now reads from an authoritative server-side lane instead of client events, so this class of silent death is measurable within a day.

A favorable pilot read is not a fix.
The push-tap dead-end fix looked 6.5x better per user on its first day at pilot volume. At full adoption the advantage decayed to none (11.7% of users on the fixed build vs 11.8% on the unfixed), while the class set three record days (peak 544 events, 92 people, Saturday). The old issue was closed, so for two days the bug had no owner; it is refiled fresh and needs a mechanism re-read this week.
