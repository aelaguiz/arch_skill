The science: where the repetition actually lives

The owner table settled it as an identity, not a correlation: the repetition is authored by our own deterministic renderer, not chosen by the model.

71.5 percent of control rows are handed the same callback sentence ("Separately, Hero misplayed preflop: say plainly that preflop was a mistake"), and the model echoes it 98.4 percent of the time.

Every output saying "preflop was a mistake" traces to an input containing that string. No exceptions in 530 rows.

Counterfactually removing that one sentence family cuts the adjacent repeat rate 60 percent (0.248 to 0.099).

So the fix went to the owning layer: one function, one sentence, four sanctioned wordings with identical meaning, chosen deterministically from the hand's own cards (no randomness, no memory, no new abstractions):

Variant | 

Wording | 

0 (unchanged) | 

"... say plainly that preflop was a mistake." | 

1 | 

"... say plainly that Hero got preflop wrong." | 

2 | 

"... say plainly that preflop went wrong." | 

3 | 

"... say plainly that the mistake was preflop." | 

A prompt-only tweak would have treated the wrong layer; the external review round is what forced the owner check before any prompt edits.

Guardrails: length within bounds, distinct frames up, exact duplicates zero, factual failures at or below the same-run control in every repeat with no new failure class, and the dominant frame changed hands exactly as predicted (to a model-invented one, which is the already-identified next treatment).

Two documented exceptions, both ruled and preserved in the artifacts

One Groq 503 out of 1,560 bench calls made the literal "100 percent accepted" leg read 259/260, so the mechanical verdict file honestly says REVERT. All 779 returned candidate generations were accepted, and the failed row re-ran clean at identical bytes. Ruling: transport availability is not copy rejection; the guardrail holds at its declared intent. The literal false stays in the artifact as evidence.

One stochastic factual draw (247/248) on a frozen-corpus row whose input bytes are identical in both arms (the change cannot reach it); six targeted repeats all passed. Ruling: provider noise, gate holds, row banked.

Bottom line against #4259: overall adjacent rate on the sealed set went from ~0.31 to ~0.20. The issue's final bar is 0.02, so this is the first crank of the machine, not the finish line. What is proven is that the machine turns.
