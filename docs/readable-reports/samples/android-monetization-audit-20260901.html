Android Monetization

What was actually broken in 2.1.39 and 2.1.40

Written September 1, 2026 575 checkout starts 45 recordings audited

Reproduction

Status: Reproduced by direct production observation.

Lane: Canonical BigQuery checkout events joined to complete PostHog mobile recordings, then checked against the exact Android release source.

What the reproduction showed:

  1. One production lesson-energy paygate showed only app-owned “Loading plans…” UI for roughly 8 to 10 seconds before the player backed out; no offering-ready event followed.
  2. A separate Play vs AI daily-limit paygate showed “This is taking longer than expected” and Retry for about 58 seconds, then recovered to pricing.
  3. One production flow exposed an internal RevenueCat and Clerk ownership error to the player for about 15 seconds. A manual action in the two-control error area was followed by recovery; the available pixels do not establish which control was tapped.
  4. Several failures returned the player to ordinary play with no visible explanation. Other explicit errors showed a clear dialog or later converted.
  5. Long static replay frames did not prove long foreground hangs. Mobile replay holds the last frame while the app is inactive or the OS owns the screen. One selected case logs app_background; another logs app_foreground on return.
  6. Release source contains a 10-second no-terminal watchdog that can put the paywall into “Checking purchase…” with its primary CTA disabled. A late result can resolve it, but the inspected path has no bounded in-place recovery action if neither native nor canonical evidence arrives. This is a credible recovery risk, not a replay-proven indefinite foreground hang.

Deployed and analyzed source: Android 2.1.39 at 7144eb88f79865d5e07264d48a48f9a9f1fe70b5; Android 2.1.40 and the detached audit worktree at f77a39bc1a53c5dadb5a664f2eadaa2264fda723.

Executive answer

Android underperformed mainly because many more checkout starts ended in cancellation, not because paywalls broadly failed to load.

  • Android purchased in 73 of 353 checkout starts, 20.7%.
  • iOS purchased in 122 of 222 starts, 55.0%.
  • The 34.3-point purchase-rate gap decomposes into 26.5 points of extra Android cancellation, 4.7 points of extra unresolved outcomes, and 3.0 points of extra explicit errors.
  • Cancellation accounts for 77.4% of the platform gap.
  • Android offerings reached ready in 881 of 889 paygate sessions, 99.1%.
  • Even if every one of the 17 final Android error outcomes had converted, Android would reach only 25.5%, still far below iOS.
  • Excluding the labeled test-market paid segment raises Android purchase rate only from 20.7% to 23.2%. That segment does not explain the gap by itself. The broader Android-versus-iOS acquisition-mix theory remains unresolved because the other acquisition differences were not normalized.

Decision

Fix the three narrow product defects and the reporting holes. Do not treat this as a systemic Android paywall-loading outage or a proven mass checkout hang. The next product question is why Android players cancel so often around the native handoff, a surface PostHog recordings cannot fully see.

Method

Cohort construction

The denominator is a distinct paygate_checkout_started session on Android or iOS version 2.1.39 or 2.1.40 during the closed Aug. 18-31 UTC window. The audit reconciled each start across:

  • subscription_checkout_store_interaction
  • paygate_result
  • paygate_checkout_finished
  • subscription_commit

A valid purchase or commit takes precedence over a conflicting later error or cancellation. A start with no canonical terminal remains unresolved. Five Android and three iOS 2.1.40 commits had no paygate_checkout_started; they were excluded from the start denominator rather than allowed to inflate conversion.

PostHog and warehouse clocks can differ by seconds, and PostHog HogQL rendered some timestamps in US Central time. Case adjudication therefore used event order plus recording-relative time, not naive timestamp equality.

Replay selection

The replay casebook included:

  • all 21 recent Android recordings with an explicit store error
  • 15 matched cancellation, unresolved, successful-purchase, offering-failure, and instrumentation controls
  • the six longest available unresolved checkout captures
  • four additional offering-problem recordings not already covered

That is 45 unique recordings because one delayed-offering recording appears in both the flow and long-unresolved sets. Forty-four were available and watched in full. One was retention-expired with a 404. The error moment for one available recording and the checkout moment for another occurred after the recording ended; those cases stay blocked rather than being guessed.

Mobile recordings were inspected frame by frame. A static frame during near-zero activity, a native billing handoff, or an explicit later app_foreground was treated as a capture limitation, not foreground-hang proof.

What is actually broken

1. Rare offering failures can block a real checkout

One 2.1.40 lesson-energy paygate showed only app-owned “Loading plans…” UI for roughly 8 to 10 seconds. The player backed out and chose Keep Learning. No offering-ready or terminal offering-error event followed.

A different 2.1.40 Play vs AI daily-limit paygate remained in app-owned loading UI for about 58 seconds. It showed “This is taking longer than expected” and Retry, then recovered to pricing. Its canonical ready event arrived 66.253 seconds after still_pending.

Neither sequence is a Google Play capture gap. Every relevant pixel belongs to the app.

The cohort also contains six 2.1.39 refresh-failure sessions. Four belonged to one player across two consecutive days. In the strongest sequence, the first failure allowed lessons to continue, but the later energy-gate failure repeated and was followed immediately by an explicit lesson quit.

Disposition: Confirmed defect, low incidence. The recovered case proves that Retry and eventual pricing can work. The abandoned case's back-out path prevented a hard trap, but no ready event followed. Neither outcome makes the delayed offering load healthy.

2. Purchase and entitlement errors are presented inconsistently

The 21 explicit error recordings contained:

Error class Recordings
Purchase not allowed 10
Store problem 6
Purchase invalid 3
Network error 1
Duplicate operation guard 1

Four affected players later committed. Some failures showed a clear, dismissible purchase dialog. Several returned silently to ordinary content. One showed an internal exact-owner entitlement message in a player-facing puzzle error surface. A tap in the two-control error area was followed by recovery, but the available pixels cannot distinguish Try again from Back to puzzles.

The direct propagation owner in 2.1.40 is /Users/aelaguiz/workspace/psmobile-worktrees/android-monetization-replay-audit-20260901/apps/flutter/lib/features/puzzles/presentation/screens/puzzles_deeplink_resolver_screen.dart:284-287, which renders _resolution.lastError?.toString() as the subtitle. The provider failure itself remains owned by the production exact-owner issue, psmobile #4484. This report confirms the separate presentation defect: internal diagnostic text escaped the error boundary.

Disposition: Confirmed player-facing error-handling defect. It is narrow and transient, but it should not reach players.

3. Pending checkout lacks a clearly bounded in-place recovery state

The release code protects against double charge and waits for late store truth, which is correct. The risk is what the player can do while that evidence never arrives.

  • The no-terminal watchdog fires after 10 seconds.
  • The service returns a pending outcome while the native future continues.
  • The journey renders SubscriptionJourneyView.checking.
  • The primary CTA reads “Checking purchase…” and is disabled.
  • A back arrow remains, but no timeout-specific Check Again or Restore action appears on the inspected paywall state.

The 31 unresolved Android starts do not prove 31 hangs. All have a store-start event and no later canonical terminal. Thirty have a recording. The six longest selected captures produced:

  • one confirmed app background after checkout
  • two visible forward-progress outcomes
  • one recording that ends 34.5 seconds into checking
  • two inactivity-dominated, relaunch-shaped gaps that do not prove foreground residence

Disposition: Unresolved recovery risk in source. The source permits an unbounded wait if neither late native truth nor canonical evidence arrives, but the replay set does not establish a systemic foreground hang.

4. Canonical monetization reporting has real gaps

These are measurement defects, not player-flow defects:

  • PostHog contains 153 Android paygate_presentation_invariant events across 2.1.39 and 2.1.40. The canonical BigQuery path contains zero.
  • Five Android and three iOS 2.1.40 purchase commits lack paygate_checkout_started.
  • One watched cancellation emitted three to four copies of paygate_opened and paygate_result, two checkout start-finish cycles, and a late cancellation-verification event for one visible player action.
  • subscription_benefits_cta records navigation from benefits to billing. It is not a store purchase click.
  • Some successful purchases lack a store-start event, so raw event-family counts cannot be treated as complete checkout populations.

Disposition: Confirmed measurement artifact. Session-grain reconciliation is required until canonical coverage is repaired.

What is not broken

Successful purchase and entitlement continuation

Four matched successful-purchase controls genuinely purchased. The captured app states never contradicted the canonical outcomes. One recovered cleanly after a full process restart. Another confirmed unlimited energy after a purchase despite a duplicate-operation guard event.

The earlier purchase-success handoff gap is real historical work, but it is resolved by the merged purchase-success fix, psmobile PR #4662. No entitlement-loss theory should be reopened from this cohort.

Most cancellation controls

The cancellation controls showed normal decline paths, native-store round trips, and continuation into free content. Static paywall frames around a later app_foreground were native or background capture gaps, not freezes.

Cancellation is still the largest business problem in the cohort. The recordings cannot show whether the player rejected the native Google Play sheet, disliked the price, lacked a valid payment setup, or simply changed their mind.

Broad offering availability

Offers reached ready in 99.1% of the closed Android paygate-session population. That disproves a broad offering outage. It does not erase the rare, directly observed failures above.

A broad missing-trial-label claim

The audited 2.1.40 screens that had trial data showed the free-trial label and renewal terms. That does not close the eligibility-specific question because no qualifying existing-subscriber plan-change recording was available.

Causal classifications

  • Pending checkout: recovery blocker, not the initiating native-store cause. The app preserves uncertainty safely but does not bound the player’s in-place wait.
  • Internal error copy: propagation defect. The exact-owner provider failure has a separate existing owner; the resolver turns internal detail into player text.
  • Offering stall: confirmed availability failure with the exact stalled provider stage unresolved for this cohort. Retry is mitigation, not proof the provider cause is repaired.
  • Canonical reporting gaps: observability defects. They distort analysis but do not cause checkout failure.

No source, production state, provider state, app data, or issue state was changed during this audit.

Recommended actions

1. Give pending checkout a bounded recovery state

Keep the double-charge guard. After a bounded evidence wait, replace the disabled-only primary action with an explicit Check again or existing Restore/reconcile action plus a safe exit. Do not re-enable Buy while purchase status is unknown.

Smallest proof:

  • one focused test where the native purchase future never completes
  • one focused test where a late purchase completes after the watchdog
  • require the first case to expose recovery without allowing a second charge
  • require the second case to grant access once and clear pending state

2. Stop rendering internal exceptions as player copy

At puzzles_deeplink_resolver_screen.dart:284-287, keep the full internal failure in structured logs and map the subtitle to one player-safe message. Preserve Retry and Back to puzzles. A plain unit or widget test should pass an exact-owner failure and assert that provider, identity, and internal class names are absent from the rendered text.

3. Preserve offering Retry, then record final disposition on the existing event

Do not add a new event family. Add one final-disposition field to the existing offering-state event with values such as recovered, backed out, refresh failed, or never ready. This separates the 4.986-second and 66.253-second recoveries from the 8-to-10-second abandonment with no ready event, without another dashboard or state machine.

4. Repair one canonical checkout model

Use distinct paygate sessions, not raw event counts. Bring the PostHog presentation-invariant family into the canonical path, reconcile late purchases with purchase precedence, and make commits-without-starts and starts-without-terminal visible as coverage warnings. Keep this in the existing dbt and Evidence surfaces.

5. Investigate cancellation at the native boundary

Use existing events first. Split cancellations by app decline before native handoff, native user cancellation, and cancellation after a store-start event. Segment by paygate surface and acquisition group. If the current terminal event cannot express that location, add one enum to it rather than another event family.

PostHog replay should remain a control for app-owned screens. It cannot tell us which Google Play plan, payment method, or native error the player saw.

What not to build

  • no global retry around a possibly live native purchase
  • no new checkout event family
  • no new replay classifier or proof pipeline
  • no broad offering rewrite
  • no reopening of the merged purchase-success handoff work

Limitations

  1. Native Google Play pixels are absent. A black, torn, or held app frame during checkout cannot prove a hang. The report uses surrounding app state and lifecycle events instead.
  2. A held mobile frame is not foreground proof. Mobile recordings preserve the last app screenshot through inactivity and OS suspension. Case 2 in the long-unresolved set logs app_foreground on return; case 3 logs app_background after checkout.
  3. Recording and event clocks are not exact twins. Event order and video-relative time carried more weight than second-level equality.
  4. One recording is unavailable. Store-error case 19 returns 404. Store-error case 18 and flow case 7 have recordings, but their assigned event occurs after the available window.
  5. The trial-badge question lacks the required account state. A trial label was visible where trial data was present, but no qualified existing-subscriber plan-change session was available.
  6. Cancellation cause remains unresolved. The population proves where the gap lands. It does not reveal what happened inside the Google Play billing sheet.

Final verdict

The Android monetization problem in these releases is not one hidden catastrophic bug. It is a large cancellation problem plus a small set of real reliability and measurement defects. Repair pending-state recovery, sanitize error copy, preserve offering recovery, and fix session-grain reporting. Then use the existing checkout events to locate cancellation around the native boundary before changing paywall design or traffic strategy.
