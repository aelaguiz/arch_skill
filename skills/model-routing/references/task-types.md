# Where each kind of work starts

Read November 17, 2026, from the evidence in [evidence.md](evidence.md). This is a dated reading, and `$model-eval` updates it. The teaching stays in `SKILL.md`, so a new result changes a row here and nothing there.

Each row gives the setting to start on from each family. Without a mark, it's the start the evidence supports: usually the cheapest setting that held up on that kind of work, where "held" means neither judge saw the score fall by much against the level above it. Where a row is a judgment call rather than a result, its last column says so. A * marks a setting that was never run on that kind: a starting guess from field use or the levels around it, tested by nothing yet. Rows rest on 3 to 6 tasks, so each is a direction. Each setting names the version it was measured on. Always run the family's newest version: a newer one starts from its row until it has been measured. A level you or your team has named wins over a row, as "Whose pick wins" says.

The rows run from work where the material carries the answer to work where the thinking is the job.

Terra 6.1 at xhigh has been run on only a few of these kinds. It cost about a third of Sol 6.1 at high and matched it on the three documents where they could be compared, so where a row says Sol 6.1 at a low level and the job is cheap to check, Terra 6.1 at xhigh is worth trying. It is weakest where the reading is the job: on saved reviewer looks it missed the most real work. It is also weaker than Sol 6.2 where a long brief carries rules no check sees: on real engineer briefs its first passes were wrong more often, so its place is mechanical work that ends in a check.

Gemini 4 Carbon (`agy`) is a third maker's family rather than a default replacement for the two columns above. Use **Gemini 4 Carbon at high** for **Second opinion, once** and bounded open analytical or systems design reads when you want an independent eye outside both Claude and Codex: on five control looks it caught the most real findings (12 of 14 on both readers) at $2.77 standard ($1.38 in November) in 13.5 minutes, and on two open systems and math questions it found verified code-level levers and bugs in 23 to 33 minutes. Do not put Carbon at high on multi-file repository helpers or code builds, where it over-reads and timed out on 3 of 5 helpers and 2 of 4 builds. When Claude or Codex pools have no room on non-build helper work (`Plan or design`, `Write short copy`, `Explain how code behaves`, `Check facts against their sources`, `Translate text`), use **Gemini 4 Carbon at medium**: it finished all 5 measured multi-file tasks and matched Opus 5.5 at xhigh on both blind judges (3.8 and 3.6 against 3.6 and 3.8) for 42% less than Opus 5.5 at xhigh at standard rates, in about a third more wall-clock time. Leave `agy` out of small fast helpers (2 to 4 minutes against under a minute), recurring watch timers (5 times Sol 6.1 at high's cost and 3 to 4 times its extra findings), and code builds while Terra 6.1, Sol 6.1 or Opus 5.5 has room.
