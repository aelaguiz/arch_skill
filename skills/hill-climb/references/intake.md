# Intake: the interview, the research and the seeding

Read this whenever the folder has no hill climb yet, or its workbook's Charter is still empty: a new climb, or one
built from earlier work. Intake ends when the owner signs off on the playback; nothing is spent before then.

Contents: why intake comes first · the shape · read first · how to ask · what the interview has to learn · earlier work
· research · follow-ups · checking you understood · the playback · seeding · when it is done

## Why intake comes first

A climb optimizes whatever it was set up to optimize, faithfully, for weeks, and every test, lesson and report inherits
that setup. Set up on a guess, it spends those weeks climbing the wrong hill. The two usual guesses are the agent's own
reading of a one-line request, and earlier work taken as settled because it is the most concrete thing in sight. A
request in a sentence leaves out what the owner is after, what they would call success and how much risk they accept;
earlier work is often an experiment its owner has already given up on. Only the owner knows these things. So before
anything runs, interview the owner until you understand each other: you can say what they want in their words, and your
guesses about their calls match theirs.

This is the one time the owner is asked for anything but money. Once the climb runs, it asks only for that.

## The shape

Read what exists; interview; research; ask follow-ups; check that you understood; play it back; seed and run the first
tick. Research raises follow-ups and follow-ups send more research, for as many rounds as it takes.

Intake can span days and restarts. Keep the Interview tab current as you go (each question as an asked row before you
send it, the made-up outcomes and the playback included, and each answer when it comes, copied exactly as the owner
wrote it, the whole reply even when it settles two questions, since your reading of it belongs in `about` or the Charter
field's reason, never in place of their words), so any session picks up from it: while the workbook's Charter is still
empty, intake is under way. Read the tab, record the owner's reply to the open question in their words, and carry on
from there; a reply that answers nothing open still goes in, word for word, with what it answers as best you can tell.

## Read first

Read the request, the folder, and enough of any earlier work to know what exists (past tests and their results,
trackers, idea lists, dimensions, rulings, research, and what is already on the platform: ads, posts or items the climb
did not make), without taking any of it on. Read what the platform offers. You read first so that no question asks what
is already written down, and so each question can come with a sound pick.

Create the workbook, every tab with its columns, before the first question (references/workbook.md;
references/charter.md, The workbook's form). The Interview tab fills as you ask, the Library as research lands, and the
rest at seeding. The owner's request is their first answer: record it as the Interview tab's first row (kind `request`),
in their words, so what it settles can cite it.

## How to ask

- **One question per message, and end your turn on it.** Asked three things at once, people answer the easiest, and two
  rows of the table below in one message are two questions, however natural the pair: what it may change and what it
  must never do, ideas and where research should look. Ask in your reply, in the conversation where the owner started
  the climb; a notification channel is for the running climb's money requests, and a question sent there waits unseen.
- **Bring what you know and your pick.** Put beside the question what the owner has said about it before, what the
  request and the files say, what the platform allows, and what you would choose and why, so most answers take a
  sentence. A high-level answer is enough: turning it into specifics is what your research is for.
- **Ask only what only the owner can answer**: what the climb is for, what it may spend, what counts as success, what
  it may and may not change. Never ask what you can read or find out.
- **Never ask the method.** How to run a hill climb (what to try first, how big a test is, the early read, how sure to
  be before acting, when a winner is replaced, how money splits between lanes) is this skill's job. Asking it hands the
  owner the work they gave you, as if they had asked you how to run a hill climb. The owner sees what the method
  produces in the playback and the reports.
- **Never ask what the climb exists to find out.** Who responds, who pays, which angle, format or offer works: asking
  makes the owner guess, and a climb told the answer tests only for the guess. Asked who the ads are for, an owner can
  only answer "whoever pays us", which is what the climb is there to find out. The same holds for "what do you think
  will work?" or "which segment should we target?". The owner's hunches are welcome as ideas to test, never as settled
  answers.

## What the interview has to learn

Word each question for the climb in front of you. When the owner has already answered one, in the request or in a ruling
of theirs in the files, do not ask it; put their answer in the playback for them to see instead. What the platform can
do is research, not their answer: a builder that takes words and pictures does not say what they want creatives made
from or which tools they will not have. An answer to one row settles another only when the owner said so: where the
climb may make changes says nothing about what it must never do. Silence is not an answer either: a request that names
only the subject lines has not said whether the send time may change, and almost no request says which tools to avoid,
so ask. The order is the usual one; follow the conversation.

| What the interview has to learn | Usually asked as | Where the answer goes |
|---|---|---|
| What the climb is for | "What is this climb for?" | `purpose` |
| How much of the earlier work to use | "How much of what we've already done should I use?" (below) | `earlier_work` |
| The number, and which way is better | "What number tells you it's working?" | `goal` |
| What counts as success or break-even | "What do we consider break-even, or success?" | `success`; you work out `bar` |
| What it may spend | "How much can it spend?" | `money_total`, `budget_unit` |
| What it may change | "What is it allowed to change?" | `levers` |
| What it must never do | "What must it never do?" | `off_limits` |
| What creatives are made from | "What do we make creatives from?" | `making_from` |
| The quality floor | "Which of our creatives should new ones be at least as good as, and what can never be wrong in them?" | `quality_floor` |
| Tools to use or avoid | "Are there tools it should use, or never use?" | `tool_rules` |
| The owner's ideas | "Any ideas you want in the mix?" | Ideas rows, source `owner` |
| Where research should look | "Where should the research look, and is there anything in particular you want researched?" | `research_direction` |
| How the owner hears from it | "How do you want to hear how it's going?" | `reporting`, `money_request_channel` |
| Whether you understood them | A couple of made-up outcomes, each with your guess of their call (below) | Interview rows, kind `outcome-check` |

Record each in the Interview tab, with the Charter fields it settles in `about`. The owner may answer "your call": then
the choice is yours, with its reason.

## Earlier work

Ask how much of it to use as soon as you have some idea what the climb is for, before any of it shapes anything. List
what exists, each piece with your read of it, for example: three tests and their results, the first of which changed
most of the ad at once; a list of 22 dimensions built around one kind of post; 102 untested ideas; a video already on
the platform that never ran; the owner's rulings from last week. The owner rules piece by piece: use it, use it as
history only, or leave it out. Until they rule, nothing from earlier work is settled, and what they leave out is used
nowhere: not as history, not as research, not in a research brief, not as a default. Work that grew by trial and error,
without a plan, is often exactly what its owner wants left behind. What they let in comes over as references/charter.md,
Seeding from earlier work, says.

## Research

After the first answers, research widely: what the interview raised, what the owner pointed to, and anywhere you can
learn from others' work, such as competitors and the category's best (and how long they kept each thing running), the
audience's own words, other markets, and the owner's other climbs (as research, never as settled answers). Research
always happens, with or without the owner's direction.

Setup also has questions of its own to research and write down where the climb uses them, each with its sources:

- **How the climb will see one item's effect on the goal**: what the platform counts for each item and how late, how
  people are tied back to the item they saw, a quick number with evidence that it tracks the goal, and a second count
  that checks the platform's (`platform`, `second_source`, `early_read`).
- **How a fair comparison runs on this platform**: how to compare two items so the platform's own delivery does not
  pick the winner, and how far the number moves by chance when nothing changes (`test_sizing`, and the method fields'
  reasons).
- **The tools that make good items now**, inside the owner's `tool_rules`, and the ones not to use (`tools`).
- **Everything an item can vary on, with examples of how each can vary**, from the medium itself, what competitors
  vary, the platform's options and the owner's items they let in (`attributes`, references/making.md).
- **The success or break-even number**, worked out from the owner's answer with each assumption written down (`bar`).
- **The material ideas draw on** (the Library, references/research.md).

Spread it across research agents, one per question, started as SKILL.md, Dispatching a role, describes for roles. Write
each brief with `$prompt-authoring`; give it what the owner said that bears on its question, and nothing they left out.

## Follow-ups

Where research changes or sharpens an answer, take it back to the owner, one question at a time, with what you found:
"The platform cannot count engaged followers for a single post; the closest it counts is follows from the post. Is
that good enough?" Repeat until the research and the answers agree.

## Checking you understood

Pick a couple of made-up outcomes where the owner's answers could point either way, write down your guess of their call,
and ask whether you are right, one outcome per message like any question: "A post gains three times the usual followers
but never mentions the product. Is that a win? My guess: only partly, because you want followers who could become
customers. Right?" Keep them about what the goal means and what the owner would accept, never about the method. Where a
guess is wrong, ask until you can say why: that gap would have steered the whole climb. Record each case and the owner's
call in the Interview tab; later decisions weigh trade-offs by them.

## The playback

Before it, go down the table: each row has the owner's answer in their words, or they left it to you; ask whatever is
missing first, even a row that looks moot, since the owner may hold a rule you cannot see (a platform with one builder
can still have a sound or a look they will not use). Then, before anything is seeded or spent, play the design back: the
owner's answers and what the research found that bears on them, each marked as the owner's words or research, plus what
to expect in the first weeks and what the climb cannot learn at this traffic. Use the form the owner said they read, or
the chat when it is short. It asks one thing: what to correct. Record it as a playback row in the Interview tab, and the
owner's reply as its answer, like any question. Correct what they correct, show them what changed, and ask whether
anything else needs correcting, until nothing does. A correction alone is not a sign-off; "fix this, otherwise go ahead"
is one: make the fix and go. That sign-off, recorded, ends the interview.

## Seeding

Then build the climb (references/charter.md for the fields, references/workbook.md for the tabs):

- the Charter: the owner's fields in their words, each citing its Interview key; setup's fields and the method fields
  with their reasons;
- the Library from the research; the Ideas, the owner's with source `owner` citing their Interview row and research's
  citing the Library; the Map;
- earlier work only as the owner ruled;
- then the first tick.

From then on the owner is asked only for money. The owner's fields change only when the owner changes them: reopen just
that question in a new Interview row, update the field, and mark in the Decisions tab where results stop being
comparable.

## When it is done

You can say what the owner wants in their words; your guesses about their calls matched; the playback left nothing to
correct; every owner's field cites the interview; and nothing from earlier work is in use that the owner did not let in.
