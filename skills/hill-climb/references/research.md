# Research: the Library ideas draw on

Read this at intake, when the scout is due, and whenever you write an idea.

## Why the Library exists

Ideas a model makes up on the spot crowd around what it already knows: asked for thousands of ideas, one model produced
only a few hundred that were not near-copies of each other. A climb fed only by invention keeps testing variations of
the same few thoughts and settles on the nearest peak. The world has already run many of the experiments you are about
to run: competitors keep the ads that make them money, a category's best performers have survived its audience, and the
audience says in its own words what it wants. The Library holds that material, so every idea starts from something that
has worked for someone, or from something the climb itself has learned.

At intake the research starts from the interview: what it raised and where the owner pointed (references/intake.md,
Research, which also lists setup's own questions). Earlier work the owner left out in `earlier_work` is not research:
it goes in no Library row and no brief.

## What goes in

- **Competitors' work**: their ads, pages, listings and offers, and how long each has been kept running.
- **The category's best**: top posts, pages and listings, with the signal that put them on top.
- **The audience's own words**: reviews, forums, comments, support messages, search phrasing.
- **Search and demand data**: what people look for and how much competition each term draws.
- **Other markets**: what worked for a similar product somewhere else.
- **The climb's own history**: the earlier work the owner let in at intake, and the climb's own tests since.

Look first where the owner pointed (`research_direction`), then where the charter's `outside_sources` says, with
whatever research tools the host has (web search, ad libraries, app-store pages, the platform's own research tools), and
record each item's `source` and `link` so anyone can go back to it.

## What makes an item evidence

An item earns its place by its **signal**: the reason to believe it says something about what works. An ad a competitor
kept running for months has survived that competitor's own testing; one that ran three days probably failed. A post with
unusual engagement for its account size found its audience. A phrase the audience repeats unprompted is their language.
A single viral item is weaker evidence than a pattern across several. Write the signal in the row, and when it is weak,
say so; the plan-checker and the strategist will weigh it.

Map each item to the cell of the map it belongs to, where it fits one, so you can see which cells the world has already
explored for you and which nobody has tried.

## How ideas draw on it

An idea names the Library rows, lessons or tests it builds on in `sources`, and its description says what it takes from
them. For a meal-kit service, say: "Two competitors have kept ads that open on a parent's tired face at 6 pm running for
over two months; we have never tested that opening." Combining items is often where the best ideas come from; inventing
freely is not. An idea that cannot name anything it draws on goes back to research. An idea the owner put in the mix
cites its Interview row; it is screened, planned and checked like any other, and research that bears on it is worth
finding.

## Keeping it fresh

Markets move: competitors change what they run, seasons turn, and platforms shift what they reward. Material goes stale
when it no longer reflects what the market is doing, sooner in a fast-moving category than a slow one. The charter's
`research_freshness` records this climb's sense of how fast that happens and why; judge each item against it and against
what you can see (a competitor's ads all replaced, a new competitor, a season change), and mark stale items `stale`:
still a record, but no longer fresh evidence. The scout refreshes the Library when its material no longer reflects the
market or when the waiting ideas would not fill what the climb could run next, and the meta-analyst watches how many
tests rest on research.
