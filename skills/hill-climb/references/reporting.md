# Progress reports

Read this when the charter's `reporting` brings a report due, and at intake, where the owner says how they want to hear.

The owner leaves the climb to run. A report is how they see it working without being asked anything, so it should let
them see in a few minutes where the climb stands and whether it is getting better, without opening the workbook.

## What a report shows

- Where the climb stands against the bar, now, over time and against where it started: the bar week by week beside the
  starting point and the best known option's number, as a small chart or table.
- What it has learned, each lesson with how sure it is (proven, unconfirmed, retired) and what it changed in the climb.
- The soft signals worth knowing: what people said and did around the items, good and bad.
- How fast it is learning: tests read, lessons proven or retired, and the money each lesson cost.
- How it is staying out of a local maximum: what exploring reached since the last report, how much of the map is still
  untried, how fresh the research is, what the meta-analyst found.
- What comes next, and the money: what is left, the pace, any request outstanding.

It asks nothing. No questions and no decisions for the owner: a money request stays its own message through
`money_request_channel`.

## How it is written

Use `$readable-reports` for a written report and `$deck-authoring` for a deck when the host has them; they hold how the
owner reads. Where their own steps would ask the owner something or say what is needed from them, the charter has
already settled it: the report's delivery note says nothing is needed. Otherwise write for a busy expert reading cold:
the answer first, things named by what they are rather than by workbook keys, every number beside what it compares to.

Put it where `reporting` says, and record it as a Reviews row with `role` `report`: the period it covers, its headline
and the link or file in `findings`, where it went in `call`, `changes` `none`. The newest such row tells the next
session when another report is due, as a role's newest Reviews row does for its cadence. The owner says in the interview
how they want reports, and that stays as they said it; when they leave it to you, `reporting` is your choice, and the
strategist may change it like any other.
