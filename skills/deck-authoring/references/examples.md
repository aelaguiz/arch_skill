# Before and after

Real slides agents built for Amir, what he said, and the version he accepted.
Each pair teaches one way of seeing; most bad slides fail several at once. Use
them to calibrate your eye, not as templates to copy.

## 1. Name every thing in full

**Before** (a deck reviewing which push notifications to switch off):
"'Keep your streak!': agree, once the 10 PM warning replaces it" with the body
"Section 8 lists it (push 48), under rule P-803." No pictures.

**He said:** "once the 10 pm warning replaces it, what 10 pm warning?... What
section 8 of what?... Make it blindingly easy."

**After:** "'Keep your streak' stops, and a 10 PM streak warning replaces it",
with a picture of today's notification and a picture of its replacement.
"Today: players with a streak who haven't played in 24 hours get it once a
day, any time from 9 AM to 10:45 PM. 1,021 went out last week. After the new
push plan goes live: a new notification at 10 PM, 'Your streak ends at
midnight'. My take: agree, if it stops the day the 10 PM warning starts."

**Why it works:** every thing the slide mentions is on the slide. A change is a
picture of today, a picture of after, and one plain why.

## 2. Write it the way he would say it

**Before:** "Fix the cap wall: 213 hit it last week, 6 saw the upgrade offer, 0
bought" and "Ship one change that moves the exactly-one pool (27% of new users)
to a second action".

**He said:** "this copy is written so fucking bad. Can you rewrite this for
human beings?"

**After:** "213 people hit the free limit last week, 6 saw the upgrade screen,
nobody bought. Fix that." and "A quarter of new users do one thing and leave.
Ship something that gets them to a second."

**Why it works:** "cap wall", "exactly-one pool", and "offer" were the agent's
labels. He says "free limit", "upgrade screen", "a quarter of new users".

## 3. List the literal jobs before any abstraction

**Before:** "Step 2: build the crosswalk", six cards of counts ("Journey terms
to the term ontology: 323 terms. 151 already match an entry by id or name; 172
need one."), then a diagram titled "The crosswalk links each item to its
partners in the other lists".

**He said:** "Put a slide before this saying, 'I literally want to do these
things: 1, 2, 3, 4.'... I don't know what the fuck you're proposing man."

**After:** "Step 2: what I literally want to do": 1. Give every journey concept
one entry in the term ontology. 2. Remove what the journey doesn't have from
the ontology and the glossary. 3. Rewrite every skill's concept list so it
names only journey concepts. 4. Point the semantic taxonomy at the journey.
"Done when every journey concept has one id."

**Why it works:** plain is not short. The accepted version has more words and
says exactly what will be done. He then argued about substance, not wording.

## 4. Show the screen, today and proposed

**Before:** "Build this week's paywalls where players run out; the lever is the
tap on Plus", over two tables of counts.

**He said:** "'The lever is the tap on Plus' means nothing to me without seeing
the UI."

**After:** kicker "Priority 3 · Needs your yes · Monetization"; headline "Out of
energy, the sheet says 'Upgrade to Plus'; we propose 'Try for $0.00'". On the
left, a real capture from the current app build labeled "Today"; on the right,
a GPT Image edit of that same capture with only the button changed, labeled
"Proposed (a mock)".

**Why it works:** a proposal about a screen is two pictures. The status lives
in the kicker.

## 5. Show the listing, not words about it

**Before:** a store landing-page proposal as seven slides of text cards, such
as "Use three page families, not five separate products" with "[PRACTICE PAGE:
poker practice / practice poker / Promise: practice real decisions]". Not one
image of a listing.

**He said:** "This is just words on a fucking screen. It doesn't propose the
listing pages. It doesn't propose the imagery."

**After:** a mockup of each page: the real title, the first-frame promise, four
real product screenshots, and the exact copy for each keyword.

**Why it works:** when the subject is visual, the slide is the visual.

## 6. One crowded slide becomes three

**Before:** "Sep 17-20: a burst of new payers we can describe, not yet
explain." Four text columns of 13 bold-lead bullets ("22 in the week.", "Same
traffic.", "Short.", "Monthly, fast.", "Poker players.") around one small chart.

**He said:** "That slide design is fucking terrible and fucking overwhelming...
Split it into multiple slides if you need to but this is overwhelming."

**After:** three slides, each with one message and its own chart: "A four-day
burst: 17 direct purchases on Sep 17-20." (a large daily bar chart); "The
buyers: mostly experienced players on the new iOS version"; "What caused it?
Four suspects, no answer yet." The caveats moved to the speaker notes.

**Why it works:** an outline he dictates is the story, not the layout. Split
before you shrink.

## 7. One change per slide: today, after, why, who, when

**Before:** a 10-row table at 8 points with rows like "One writer of SKAN
values | AppsFlyer, plus Meta's SDK reporter at its default | LATER", and a
board of 7.5-point cards.

**He said:** "I can't understand what you're saying to do... break this up
into several slides".

**After:** ten slides, one change each. "Meta's checkout event moves from the
paywall to the tap on buy." TODAY: "When a player sees the paywall, AppsFlyer
tells Meta the player started a checkout. When a player actually taps buy,
Meta hears nothing." AFTER THE CHANGE: "The tap on buy reaches Meta as
InitiateCheckout." Then WHY IT MATTERS, WHO DOES IT ("You: decide. Me: add the
two rows"), and WHEN ("The morning you say go"). He approved them one by one.

**Why it works:** a table that packs recommendations into rows hides each one.
Full sentences per change, with today beside after, are readable at a glance.

## 8. The answer first, simple enough to act on

**Before:** slide 2, "THE ANSWER · Ship each language on both stores the day it
ships; let an agent keep it", with five numbered moves, each with a two-line
justification at 8.8 points.

**He said:** "Is that the simple version of the proposal?... It should be
really fucking simple."

**After:** "TL;DR · Build one skill. Amir runs it every time the store listing
is updated." Three large boxes: "1 The listing is updated" then "2 Amir runs
the skill: one command, nothing else to do" then "3 Every language is done".
Then "The end." and "Everything after this slide is the why and the detail."

**Why it works:** the front answers the question in one look. The reasoning
follows for anyone who wants it.

## 9. Divide a long deck into parts

**Before:** a 36-slide pricing plan deck as one continuous run of content
slides.

**He said:** "can you save a repo rule that in slide decks we always break up
the flow with subsection dividers? It's too much"

**After:** the deck grouped by the question each part answers, each part opened
by a divider with its number, its plain name, one sentence on what it answers,
and its slides with page numbers. The ten slides on running the price test
became two subsections, "setting it up" and "deciding and reading", each with
its own divider.

**Why it works:** a divider is a pause and a map. He knows which part he is in,
what it is for, and where to jump.

## 10. Units, periods, and baselines

**Before:** a chart guide line labeled "50 / ad set"; a budget table with
columns "At today's cost" and "Assume 25% lower cost".

**He said:** "these numbers don't say per what. 50 / ad set...per day? week?
year/" and "add at March's cost too as a starting column (left of today's
cost)".

**After:** "Events per week", with the line "50 events per ad set per week";
columns March, then today, then the assumed improvement.

**Why it works:** a number without its unit, period, and baseline is not yet
information.

## 11. The upside as a comparison he can feel

**Before:** "We're below benchmark on every money step", with "reaching the
median alone takes payers per install from 0.9% to 2.15%".

**He said:** "rather than 'we're below benchmark,' is just that we have a lot
to gain from optimizing... how many more payers we'd be getting today".

**After:** "Why now: we have a lot to gain from optimizing", with three bars of
new payers a month at today's install volume: today about 75, at the median
180, at the top quarter 400.

**Why it works:** the comparison is in the unit he decides with, payers, and it
is framed as the opportunity.

## 12. The headline states the finding and its number

**Before:** "Lower return rates don't rule out an exclusion problem", with two
cards and a footer, "The exclusion concern includes active users. This return
metric misses them."

**He said:** "Put it like that's almost the headline: the return rate will
always fall because it's out of the dormant device pool, which is growing."

**After:** "The rate fell because the dormant pool grew faster than returns",
annotated "Rate -63% · returns +39% · pool +274%".

**Why it works:** a headline that only says what cannot be ruled out tells him
nothing. State the arithmetic.

## 13. The kicker is a label

**Before:** kicker "2B · How do we monetize non-paying users?" over the
headline "In short: we don't."

**He said:** "Don't use the little title at the very top as part of the
content."

**After:** kicker "2B · Non-paying users"; headline "We don't make any money
from non-paying users yet." About 30 headlines in that deck were rewritten the
same way.

**Why it works:** readers skip the kicker. The headline must stand alone.

## 14. Say what kind of slide it is

**Before:** "If Meta stops: $140 a week to Apple US, $147 to a language test,
$170 kept" and "Three edits in Poker Training Test, undone the same way".

**He said:** "are you recommending that we stop it? Are you explaining a
hypothetical? What's the actual claim?" and "Am I being asked to undo edits?"

**After:** kicker "Needs your yes"; "Pause Meta's two campaigns: saves about
$525 a week, costs about 1 payer a week", followed by "The three settings,
before and after, and how we'd roll them back". He answered "yes".

**Why it works:** the title is the action; the kicker is the status. He knows
at once whether he is being informed, asked, or shown a hypothetical.

## 15. Check it, then state it

**Before:** "iPhone's measurement has to be fixed before we test signals
there", with "META'S OTHER IPHONE ROUTE IS IN DOUBT: one record says never set
up, another saw it eligible on September 5."

**He said:** "If there's stuff you need to go check, then just go fucking check
it. Don't tell me it needs to get checked."

**After:** "iPhone runs on Meta's own measurement, and it works", with a bar
chart of what Meta counted and the note "SKAN was switched off on purpose.
Nothing broke."

**Why it works:** a slide carries findings, not to-dos for him.

## 16. The same words, a real picture

**Before:** "Their whole business is one leg of our stool." over a bar reading
"ONE ONGOING POKER RELATIONSHIP" and three dark text boxes: "LEARN / Understand
the game.", "PUZZLES / Test a decision.", "PLAY / With others or against AI."
Most slides in that deck used the same headline, subline, and three-box
layout.

**He said:** "They are just highly repetetive so your brain glazes over you
can't see a visual shift from slide to slide."

**After:** the same headline and the same lines beside an illustration of a
three-legged stool with its legs labeled Learn, Puzzles, Play. Across the
redesigned deck the median words per slide barely moved (about 40 before, 43
after); what changed was that each slide got its own visual form: an
illustration, a bar chart for a price ladder, one giant number, proportional
circles for two market sizes, real competitor screens.

**Why it works:** short words were not enough. The idea landed when he could
see it, and the deck held attention when the form changed with the idea.

## 17. Cut to what matters for this deck

**Before:** five table slides listing 38 features, with cells such as
"Glossary | Live. Free." and "Tournaments | Not built."

**He said:** "stop saying the word 'live' over and over again... make this one
slide. You exhaustively listed every fucking feature as opposed to just the
ones that fucking matter."

**After:** one slide, "The features that shape our economy, today and in the
next version", with eight rows such as "Lessons | Energy: 1 per correct step,
20 max, about 30 hours to refill", and no status words.

**Why it works:** the list is scoped to the deck's question, and nothing on it
is something he already knows.

## 18. Make the change visible at a glance

**Before:** "Intended journey at a glance" drawn in the same five-box layout as
today's journey, with the changes only in small subtext.

**He said:** "The differences between 9 and 11 are not clear enough."

**After:** each change as large cyan text in its box ("Coached hand first",
"Says back their goals", "A reward and a next step", "A personal journey"),
the stage names small above, the unchanged stage dimmed and dashed, and one
legend line. He later pointed agents at this slide as the model for drawing a
journey.

**Why it works:** the eye lands on what changes.
